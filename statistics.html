How much data did the model learn from?

512,381 real flights over a full year, to and from 110 airports.

The dataset covers every departure from Istanbul Airport (IST) over 365 consecutive days. Each flight record includes the airline, destination, aircraft type, scheduled time, and whether it was delayed.

More about the dataset

A flight is counted as "delayed" if it departed 15 or more minutes after its scheduled time. The data was collected from public flight-tracking sources and cleaned to remove cancelled flights and entries with missing fields.

How often are flights delayed?

About 1 in 7 flights — 13.8% of all departures were delayed.

Out of 512,381 flights, roughly 70,700 experienced a delay of 15 minutes or more.
Why this matters for the model

When only 13.8% of flights are delayed, the dataset is imbalanced — there are far more on-time flights than delayed ones. This makes it harder for the model to learn what a "delayed" flight looks like, and it affects metrics like precision.

How accurate is the model?

It correctly identifies 66 out of every 100 flights.

What do these metrics mean?

Accuracy (0.6559): The model gets the right answer 65.59% of the time.

AUC-ROC (0.7798): Measures how well the model separates delayed from on-time flights. 0.5 is random guessing, 1.0 is perfect. 0.78 is a decent score.

Precision (0.0717): Of all the flights the model flagged as "will be delayed," only 7.17% actually were. The low precision means many false alarms.

Recall (0.6177): Of all flights that really were delayed, the model caught 61.77% of them.

F1 Score (0.6207): A balance between precision and recall.

MCC (0.6407): Matthews Correlation Coefficient — a balanced measure that accounts for all four outcomes (true/false positives/negatives).

What matters most for predicting delays?

When the flight is scheduled matters more than where it is going.

How feature importance works

Feature importance shows how much each input variable contributes to the model's predictions. A higher score means that feature has more influence on whether the model predicts a delay. The model uses a gradient boosting algorithm that naturally ranks features by how useful they are for splitting decisions.
